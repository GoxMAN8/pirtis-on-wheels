English way, a too-many check that came after the name, "which dates are free" read as a

call**: the agent remembered what was said but not where the conversation stood, so it
didn't close after a finished task, repeated a refusal word for word, lost its last
question after a check-in, and asked "Shall I book it?" again after "book it".

## What went wrong, by cause, and what was done

| Case | Cause | Fixed by |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | a finished booking left the call open | every finished task ends "Is there anything else I can help with?", and "no, that's all" closes the call politely (D-98) |
| C2 | the same refusal, word for word, after the same answer | a refusal we just gave closes the topic the second time instead of asking again (D-98) |
| C3b | "yes, I'm here" after a check-in got a generic reply | the gate brings back the last question: "Great. Which dates were you thinking of?" (D-98) |
| C4b | "which dates are free?" was no longer a price question (D-95), but got a question back | answered with the coming weekends and how many saunas are free (D-99); later extended to a named month and a named sauna (D-100) |
| C8 | "book it" to an offer we had just read back was taken as a go-ahead, not an acceptance | to our own read-back, either counts as a yes (D-98) |

All five come from one gap: the transcript was the agent's only memory, and it read the
caller's lines but not its own. Reading its own last line fixed them without adding any
state; the memory model is written up in
[how the receptionist remembers](../../docs/architecture/logic-flow.md#how-the-receptionist-remembers).
